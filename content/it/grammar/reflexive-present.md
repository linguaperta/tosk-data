# Reflexive verbs: mi alzo

Many everyday verbs are **reflexive** (pronominal): the action comes back to the subject. In the dictionary they end in **-si**: *alzarsi* (to get up), *chiamarsi* (to be called), *sentirsi* (to feel). The *si* becomes a pronoun **before** the conjugated verb, matching the subject.

| | pronoun | alzarsi | sentirsi |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | mi | mi alzo | mi sento |
| tu | ti | ti alzi | ti senti |
| lui/lei | si | si alza | si sente |
| noi | ci | ci alziamo | ci sentiamo |
| voi | vi | vi alzate | vi sentite |
| loro | si | si alzano | si sentono |

- The verb itself is conjugated as usual: *mi alzo* like *alzo*, *mi vesto* like *vesto*.
- *Non* comes before the pronoun: *Non mi sento bene.* (I don't feel well.)
- After a modal verb the pronoun can go first or be attached to the infinitive, matching the person: *Mi devo alzare.* = *Devo alzarmi.* (I have to get up.)
- Many are not "-self" in English: *mi chiamo Anna* (my name is Anna), *mi annoio* (I'm bored), *si arrabbia* (he gets angry), *ci divertiamo* (we have fun).
- With a plural subject they can mean "each other": *Ci vediamo domani.* (See you tomorrow.)
- *Andarsene* (to go away) has two pronouns: *me ne vado, te ne vai, se ne va, ce ne andiamo, ve ne andate, se ne vanno*.
