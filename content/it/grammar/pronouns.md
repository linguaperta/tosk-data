# Object pronouns

Italian has short (unstressed) pronouns for "me, you, him, it, us, them". They go **before the conjugated verb**: *Ti vedo.* (I see you.) *Mi piace.* (I like it, literally "it pleases me".)

| | direct (whom? what?) | indirect (to whom?) | stressed (after a preposition) |
|---|---|---|---|
| me | mi | mi | me |
| you | ti | ti | te |
| him / it | lo | gli | lui |
| her / it | la | le | lei |
| us | ci | ci | noi |
| you (plural) | vi | vi | voi |
| them | li (m) / le (f) | gli | loro |

- *Lo* and *la* become *l'* before a vowel or *h*: *L'ho visto.* (I saw him.)
- Negation comes first: *Non lo so.* (I don't know it.)
- After an infinitive the pronoun is attached to it: *Voglio vederti.* = *Ti voglio vedere.*
- **si** is the reflexive "himself, herself, themselves" (*si chiama* = he/she is called) and also impersonal "one, people" (*Qui si mangia bene.*).
- **ci** also means "there" (*Ci vado domani.* = I'm going there tomorrow; *c'è* = there is). **ne** means "of it, of them, some": *Ne voglio due.* (I want two of them.)
- After a preposition use the stressed forms: *con me, per te, da lui*.
