# Si dice, si mangia: impersonal si

**si** + a verb in the 3rd person means "one, you, people" in general: there is no specific subject.

- *In Italia si mangia bene.* (In Italy you eat well / people eat well.)
- *Come si dice "thank you" in italiano?* (How do you say ...?)
- *Qui non si fuma.* (No smoking here.)

**Passive si**: with a noun the verb agrees with it, singular or plural:

- *Si vende una casa.* (A house is for sale.)
- *Si vendono case.* (Houses for sale.)
- *Qui si parlano tre lingue.* (Three languages are spoken here.)

Do not confuse it with the reflexive *si* (*Marco si alza* = Marco gets up), where there is a subject.

Other impersonal verbs, used in the 3rd person: **basta** (that's enough: *Basta così!*), **capita** (it happens: *Capita a tutti.*), **accade** (it happens, more formal), **vale la pena** (it's worth it), **si tratta di** (it's about: *Si tratta di un errore.*). With a reflexive verb the impersonal form is **ci si**: *Ci si diverte.* (You have fun.)
