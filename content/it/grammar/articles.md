# The definite article

Every Italian noun is masculine or feminine. The definite article ("the") depends on the gender, on singular or plural, and on the first sound of the word that follows.

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine, most consonants | **il** libro | **i** libri |
| masculine, s + consonant, z, gn, ps, x, y | **lo** studente, **lo** zio | **gli** studenti, **gli** zii |
| masculine, vowel | **l'**amico | **gli** amici |
| feminine, consonant | **la** casa | **le** case |
| feminine, vowel | **l'**acqua | **le** acque |

- Nouns ending in **-o** are usually masculine (*il ragazzo*), nouns in **-a** usually feminine (*la ragazza*). Nouns in **-e** can be either: *il padre*, *la madre*, *il pane*, *la notte*.
- Exceptions to learn: *la mano* (hand), *il problema*, *il caffè*, *l'uomo* (plural *gli uomini*).
- *L'* hides the gender: *l'amico* (m) and *l'amica* (f). The plural shows it again: *gli amici*, *le amiche*.
- Indefinite article: *un* (un libro, un amico), *uno* (uno studente, uno zio), *una* (una casa), *un'* (un'amica).
- This skill asks for the singular definite article: *il*, *lo*, *la* or *l'*.
