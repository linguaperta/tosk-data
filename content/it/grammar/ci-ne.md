# Ci and ne: ci vado, ne voglio due

Two small pronouns that replace a whole phrase.

**ci** replaces a place (*a/in* + place) or *a* + a thing:

- *Vai a Roma? Sì, ci vado domani.* (Yes, I'm going there tomorrow.)
- *Pensi all'esame? Sì, ci penso sempre.* (I always think about it.)
- *Non ci riesco.* (I can't manage it.) *Ci credo.* (I believe it.)
- Fixed verbs with *ci*: **volerci** (to take, to be needed): *Ci vuole un'ora.* *Ci vogliono due ore.* **metterci** (to take, of a person): *Ci metto dieci minuti.* **farcela** (to manage): *Ce la faccio!* **pensarci** (to think about it, to see to it): *Ci penso io.* **crederci** (to believe in it): *Non ci credo!* **tenerci** (to care about it): *Ci tengo molto.*

**ne** replaces *di* + something, and a noun after a number or quantity:

- *Quante mele vuoi? Ne voglio due.* (I want two of them.)
- *Hai del pane? Sì, ne ho un po'.* (Yes, I have some.)
- *Che ne pensi?* (What do you think about it?) *Non ne parliamo più.* (Let's not talk about it any more.)

Both go before the conjugated verb, like the other pronouns, and attach to an infinitive or imperative: *Devo andarci.* *Prendine due!* Before *è*, *ci* becomes *c'*: *c'è* (there is).
