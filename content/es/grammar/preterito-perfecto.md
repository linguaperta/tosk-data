# The perfect: he comido

The **pretérito perfecto** is formed with the present of **haber** plus the **participle**. It is used for things that happened in a period that is not over yet (*hoy, esta semana, este año*), for experiences (*alguna vez, nunca*) and with *ya* and *todavía no*.

| | hablar | comer | vivir |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | he hablado | he comido | he vivido |
| tú | has hablado | has comido | has vivido |
| él / ella / usted | ha hablado | ha comido | ha vivido |
| nosotros | hemos hablado | hemos comido | hemos vivido |
| vosotros | habéis hablado | habéis comido | habéis vivido |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | han hablado | han comido | han vivido |

- Participles: **-ar → -ado** (hablado), **-er / -ir → -ido** (comido, vivido).
- All verbs use *haber*, also *ir* and *venir*: *He ido al cine.* (I have been to the cinema.)
- The participle never changes here: *Ana ha llegado.* *Ellas han comido.*
- Nothing comes between *haber* and the participle: *Ya he comido.* *No lo he visto.* Pronouns and *no* go before *haber*.
- *Hoy he trabajado mucho* (Spain). With *ayer* or *el año pasado* Spain uses the simple past (indefinido): *Ayer trabajé mucho*. In most of Latin America the simple past is also common for today.
