# Object pronouns: lo, la, le

Object pronouns stand **before** the conjugated verb.

| | direct (whom/what) | indirect (to whom) |
|---|---|---|
| me | me | me |
| you | te | te |
| him / her / it / you (usted) | lo, la | le |
| us | nos | nos |
| you (plural) | os | os |
| them / you (ustedes) | los, las | les |

- Direct: *¿Ves a Ana? – Sí, la veo.* *El libro: lo compro.*
- Indirect: *Le doy el libro.* (I give him/her the book.) *Les escribo.* (I write to them.)
- Both together: indirect first, and *le/les* becomes **se**: *Se lo doy.* (I give it to him.)
- *No* comes before the pronoun: *No lo sé.* (I don't know.)
- After an infinitive, a gerund or a positive command they are attached: *Quiero verlo.* *¡Dímelo!*
- In much of Spain people say *le* for a male person as direct object too: *Le veo* = *Lo veo* (I see him).
