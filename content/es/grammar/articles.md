# The article: el, la

Every Spanish noun is **masculine** or **feminine**. The article shows which:

| | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| the (singular) | el libro | la casa |
| the (plural) | los libros | las casas |
| a / some | un libro, unos libros | una casa, unas casas |

- Most nouns in **-o** are masculine (*el vino*) and most in **-a** are feminine (*la mesa*). Exceptions: *la mano, el día, el problema, el mapa*.
- Nouns in **-ción, -dad, -tad** are feminine (*la canción, la ciudad*). Others, like *-e* or a consonant, you learn with the article: *el pan, la leche, la sal*.
- Feminine nouns starting with a stressed *a-* take **el** in the singular to sound better: *el agua, el hambre*. They stay feminine: *el agua fría*, *las aguas*.
- Some people nouns have one form for both: *el/la estudiante*.
- Learn every noun together with its article.
