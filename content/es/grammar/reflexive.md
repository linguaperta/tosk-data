# Reflexive verbs: me levanto

Reflexive verbs have a pronoun that refers back to the subject. In the dictionary they end in **-se**: *levantarse* (to get up), *llamarse* (to be called).

| | levantarse |
|---|---|
| yo | **me** levanto |
| tú | **te** levantas |
| él / ella / usted | **se** levanta |
| nosotros | **nos** levantamos |
| vosotros | **os** levantáis |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **se** levantan |

- The pronoun goes before the conjugated verb, or is attached to an infinitive or gerund: *Me voy a duchar* = *Voy a ducharme*.
- Daily routine: *despertarse* (to wake up), *levantarse*, *ducharse*, *lavarse* (to wash), *vestirse* (to get dressed, *e → i*), *acostarse* (to go to bed, *o → ue*), *sentarse* (to sit down, *e → ie*).
- Many verbs change meaning with *se*: *quedar* (to meet) / *quedarse* (to stay); *ir* (to go) / *irse* (to leave); *llamar* (to call) / *llamarse* (to be called).
- Body parts take the article, not a possessive: *Me lavo las manos.* (I wash my hands.)
- *Nos* and *se* can also mean "each other": *Nos vemos mañana.* *Se quieren mucho.*
- *Se* also makes impersonal sentences: *Aquí se habla español.* (Spanish is spoken here.)
- The exercises practise the verb forms; the pronoun stays the same as in the table above.
