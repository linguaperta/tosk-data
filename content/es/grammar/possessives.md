# My, your, his: possessives

The short forms go **before** the noun and agree only in number (*nuestro* and *vuestro* also in gender):

| | one thing | several things |
|---|---|---|
| my | mi casa | mis casas |
| your | tu casa | tus casas |
| his / her / its / your (usted) | su casa | sus casas |
| our | nuestro coche, nuestra casa | nuestros coches, nuestras casas |
| your (plural) | vuestro coche, vuestra casa | vuestros coches, vuestras casas |
| their / your (ustedes) | su casa | sus casas |

- *Tu* (your) has no accent; *tú* (you) has one.
- *Su* can mean his, her, its, their or your (formal). If it is unclear, say *la casa de él / de ella / de usted*.
- The long forms come **after** the noun or stand alone: *mío, tuyo, suyo, nuestro, vuestro*. They agree like adjectives: *Es mío.* *La culpa es mía.* *un amigo mío* (a friend of mine).
- No article with the short forms: *mi libro* (not *el mi libro*), but *el mío* = mine.
