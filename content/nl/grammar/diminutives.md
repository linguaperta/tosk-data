# Diminutives

Dutch uses diminutives all the time: for small things (*een huisje*, a little house), to sound friendly (*een kopje koffie*, *een biertje*) and in fixed words (*een meisje*, *een broodje*). A diminutive is **always a het-word**, and its plural takes **-s**: *het huisje → de huisjes*.

| ending | when | example |
|---|---|---|
| **-je** | after most consonants (*b, d, f, g, k, p, s, t, ch*) | huis → huis**je**, boek → boek**je**, kind → kind**je** |
| **-tje** | after a vowel, a long vowel + *l, n, r*, or *-el, -en, -er* | ei → ei**tje**, stoel → stoel**tje**, tafel → tafel**tje**, deur → deur**tje** |
| **-pje** | after a long vowel + *m*, or *-em, -um* | boom → boom**pje**, bloem → bloem**pje** |
| **-etje** | after a short vowel + *b, l, m, n, ng, r* (the consonant doubles) | man → mann**etje**, bal → ball**etje**, ring → ring**etje** |
| **-kje** | after unstressed *-ing* (the *g* becomes *k*) | koning → konin**kje** |

- Words ending in *a, o, u* double their vowel: *auto → autootje*, *oma → omaatje*.
- Some words are diminutives with a meaning of their own: *meisje* (girl), *broodje* (bread roll), *kopje* (cup).
