# Plurals

Most Dutch nouns take **-en** or **-s** in the plural. The article is always *de*: *het huis → de huizen*.

| ending | when | example |
|---|---|---|
| **-en** | most nouns, especially one-syllable words | boek → boek**en**, vrouw → vrouw**en** |
| **-s** | after unstressed *-el, -em, -en, -er, -je* | tafel → tafel**s**, jongen → jongen**s**, meisje → meisje**s** |
| **'s** | after *a, i, o, u, y* | auto → auto**'s**, baby → baby**'s** |
| **-s** | after *-e* (often also *-n*) | koffie → koffie**s**, groente → groente**n** / groente**s** |

- **Spelling** keeps the vowel sound: a short vowel doubles the consonant (*kat → katten*, *zus → zussen*); a long vowel in a closed syllable loses a letter (*boom → bomen*, *uur → uren*).
- **f → v** and **s → z** after a long vowel or a consonant: *brief → brieven*, *huis → huizen*. Not always: *kous → kousen*.
- Some nouns change their vowel: *stad → steden*, *schip → schepen*, *dag → dagen* (short *a* stays single).
- A few take **-eren**: *kind → kinderen*, *ei → eieren*.
- Both endings are sometimes right: *appels / appelen*, *groenten / groentes*. Mass nouns such as *melk* or *fruit* have no plural in this course.
